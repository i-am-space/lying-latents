%=======================================================
\begin{abstract}
%=======================================================
Interpretability research often claims that a particular sparse autoencoder (SAE) latent drives a language
model's behaviour. With thousands of latents to choose from, some will look important by chance, and a
latent that tracks a concept need not be one the model uses. We audit the standard ways of selecting latents
on three models and nine datasets. With a linear probe on pooled latents, reusing the same data to search
and validate certifies pure noise in up to 100\% of experiments. Model-X knockoffs, which compare each
latent with a statistically matched fake copy, are detectably mismatched on every dataset and can exceed
their promised false discovery rate (FDR) when labels depend on interactions between latents. A hurdle sampler
repairs the zeros, but when we intervene on the latents knockoffs pick, few have a meaningful effect on the model. We therefore test the causal question directly:
we change each candidate latent inside the model and ask whether the answer moves by more than a minimum
meaningful amount, with an FDR guarantee that makes no assumption about how latents are distributed. On two
models and four tasks, realised FDR never exceeded 0.003 at up to 2,000 inputs. On the held-out model (Gemma-3), power (the share of
latents with a meaningful effect that the test finds) was 0.34--0.66 at 2,000 inputs. Most of the 250 screened
latents per task have a small effect, but only 1--9 have a meaningful one.
\end{abstract}

%=======================================================
\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
%=======================================================

Sparse autoencoders (SAEs) rewrite a language model's activations as sparse combinations of thousands of
learned directions, its \emph{latents}, many of which appear to track one concept
\citep{cunningham2023sparse, bricken2023monosemanticity, templeton2024scaling}. Claims built on them,
such as ``latent $j$ encodes sentiment and the model uses it'', are statistical claims, but they rarely
come with an error rate. A common recipe, \emph{search-then-validate}, ranks latents by association with a label,
keeps the top few and checks that removing them changes a prediction; ablation itself is a standard
validation tool \citep{wang2023interpretability, marks2025sparse}. It tests thousands
of latents without multiplicity correction \citep{benjamini1995controlling} and often selects and
validates on the same data \citep{kriegeskorte2009circular}.

Model-X knockoffs \citep{candes2018panning}, applied to SAE latents with Gaussian decoys by
\citet{enkhbayar2025}, control the false discovery rate (FDR) if latents and decoys are exchangeable.
SAE latents are non-negative, exactly zero on most inputs and co-fire nonlinearly; Gaussian decoys are
never zero. We show the violation is detectable on every dataset, can produce FDR breaches in interaction designs, and
is reduced but not removed by better samplers. Moreover, even a valid knockoff answers an associational question, while interpretability
claims are causal. Interventions on our own model are exact and repeatable and cost only compute (26--527 minutes per task here), so we test the causal
question directly.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item An audit of SAE feature selection on 3 models and 9 datasets, covering Gaussian, hurdle and learned knockoffs
        and e-value splitting (\S\ref{sec:audit}).
  \item A downstream check that scores every latent knockoffs select against measured effects (\S\ref{sec:downstream}).
  \item Interventional e-BH with an absolute minimum-effect null (\S\ref{sec:method}).
  \item Validation against measured effects on two real models, a comparison with zero-effect and relative nulls,
        and the finding that few screened latents matter on their own (\S\ref{sec:results}).
\end{enumerate}

%=======================================================
\section{Background and Related Work}
\label{sec:background}
%=======================================================

\paragraph{SAEs.} JumpReLU SAEs \citep{rajamanoharan2024jumping, lieberum2024gemma} give each latent a
firing threshold, so its activations are a point mass at zero plus a positive part. SAE latents split
and absorb concepts \citep{chanin2024absorption}, score well even on random transformers
\citep{heap2025random}, and trail simple baselines on probing and steering
\citep{kantamneni2025sparse, wu2025axbench}; SAEBench evaluates SAEs as a whole
\citep{karvonen2025saebench}.

\paragraph{Interventions.} Components are validated by ablation or patching
\citep{wang2023interpretability, heimersheim2024patching}, which is sensitive to the metric
\citep{zhang2024towards} and to off-distribution edits \citep{makelov2024illusion}. Attribution patching
approximates many interventions with one gradient \citep{syed2023attribution, kramar2024atp} and is used
to select SAE latents \citep{marks2025sparse}. None of these controls the error rate of the reported set.

\paragraph{Multiple testing.} FDR is the expected share of false selections
\citep{benjamini1995controlling}; the Benjamini--Yekutieli (BY) multiple-testing correction controls it under any
dependence \citep{benjamini2001control}. Selecting hypotheses from the data that tests them is selective inference
\citep{berk2013valid, taylor2015statistical}.

\paragraph{Knockoffs.} A latent is null if it says nothing about the label $Y$ once all other latents are known.
Model-X knockoffs \citep{candes2018panning} build, without looking at $Y$, a fake copy of each latent (its
\emph{knockoff}). The copies must be \emph{exchangeable}: swapping any latents with their knockoffs leaves the joint
distribution unchanged. A latent is selected if it predicts the label clearly better than its fake in a sparse
logistic fit. A null latent and its fake are equally likely to win, so counting the fakes' wins estimates the false
selections, and the Knockoff+ threshold \citep{barber2015controlling} uses this to keep FDR at most $q$
(Appendix~\ref{app:planted}). Inexact knockoffs inflate FDR by at most a KL gap \citep{barber2020}. Samplers include
second-order Gaussian knockoffs, which match only means and covariances (equicorrelated, MVR, and block MVR on blocks of at
most 512 latents; \citealp{spector2022powerful}); SCIP, which draws each knockoff from its conditional distribution in turn
\citep{candes2018panning}; its Metropolized version \citep{bates2021metropolized}; and deep samplers \citep{romano2020deep}.

\paragraph{E-values.} An e-value is a nonnegative score that averages at most 1 under the null, so a large value is
evidence against it. e-BH \citep{wang2022evalues} turns $K$ e-values into a list of discoveries with FDR at most $\alpha$
under any dependence. Betting builds an e-value for a bounded mean \citep{waudbysmith2024betting}: a gambler starting
with wealth 1 repeatedly bets that the mean exceeds the null value. Under the null the expected wealth does not grow, and
the final wealth is the e-value.

\paragraph{SAE-specific work.} \citet{enkhbayar2025} applies Gaussian knockoffs to 512 high-activity latents of a Pythia-70M
SAE on SST-2 and selects 129 at $q = 0.1$, assuming the exchangeability that \S\ref{sec:audit} tests. \citet{khera2026}
compares zero-ablating a latent with zero-ablating a random one, using a betting e-process per latent and e-BH, and finds 6 latents
in GPT-2 Small on indirect-object identification, where knockoffs find none. We use the same e-value tools, concurrently,
but with an absolute minimum effect, and we compare the two in \S\ref{sec:results}. \citet{shi2024hypothesis} test a whole
circuit rather than single latents. \citet{guo2026discovery} show that observational discovery evidence does not determine an
intervention's first-order effect. \citet{bal2026} finds that 9--14\% of SAE features matching a known direction are
causally inert in trained SAEs, and up to 77\% in a degraded one.

A typical latent is exactly zero on 89\% of SST-2 sentences and on 30--73\% of SAEBench texts; Gaussian knockoffs never

\citealp{gretton2012kernel}). Any atom also makes the KL term in the robustness bound infinite \citep{barber2020}.

The hurdle sampler \citep{mullahy1986specification} models whether a latent fires and how strongly; it

(Gaussian: 0.49). It gave up the exact covariance match. A \emph{second-order-exact} version, whose knockoffs match the covariance exactly, restores it, but its learned part is
about 1\% of the noise and it behaves like block MVR. FDR is a joint property of 2,048 dependent latents,
and no sampler we built can be certified jointly.

\subsection{The valid fallback is costly}
\label{sec:evalue-obs}

E-value splitting (lasso selection on one half, a logistic refit on the other, e-BH) never breached
(14 model--dataset settings, FDR 0.000--0.008) but on SAEBench has 0.27--0.46 less power than hurdle knockoffs (0.60 on the smallest dataset); on SST-2 the gap is only 0.02 (0.952 vs.\ 0.974).
Its null $z$-scores are over-dispersed (variance 1.69), so its validity is empirical
(Appendix~\ref{app:evalue}).

\paragraph{Summary.} None of these observational methods has a guarantee we can certify on SAE latents, and all target
association with a label rather than effect on the model.

%=======================================================
\section{Do Knockoff Picks Matter to the Model?}
\label{sec:downstream}
%=======================================================

The audit asks whether knockoffs keep their promise. Our downstream check asks whether the latents they select
are ones the model uses. We use direct interventions on prompted classification tasks rather than steering, because
there every latent's effect on the answer can be measured, which gives a ground
truth for each candidate.

\paragraph{Target.} For a prompted classification task, $m(x)$ is the model's probability of the correct answer on input
$x$, normalised over the label tokens. The effect of latent $j$ on $x$ is how much this drops when we intervene on $j$:
\begin{equation}
\Delta_j(x) = m(x) - m(x;\ \text{intervene on } j) \in [-1, 1].
\end{equation}
With $\tauj = \mathbb{E}_x[\Delta_j(x)]$, we test $H_j: \tauj \le \delta$, where $\delta$ is the smallest drop we count as
meaningful: $0.05\,\mathrm{SD}(m)$ on a separate pilot split, 0.008--0.016, about one percentage point. A null of $\tauj
\le 0$ would not do, because almost every latent moves the output slightly (\S\ref{sec:results}). The claim is causal
within the model's computation, for these prompts; redundant latents can be missed.

\paragraph{Intervention.} Let $h$ be the residual-stream activation after the SAE's layer (Table~\ref{tab:models}) at one
token. The SAE writes $h$ as latent activations $a_j$ times decoder directions $d_j$, plus an error term. To intervene we
replace $a_j$ with $\tilde a_j$ and set $h \leftarrow h + (\tilde a_j - a_j)\, d_j$, leaving everything else unchanged, on every
query token, including the template around the input (``Review:'', ``Sentiment:'') but not the instruction or demonstrations.
The primary $\tilde a_j$ is \emph{resampling}: a realistic value drawn from a token-level hurdle model of $a_j$ given its 64
most co-active latents (Appendix~\ref{app:stage5}). Zero ablation and mean ablation are sensitivity analyses. Validity does
not depend on this sampler. We rerun only the layers after the edit, which matches the full forward pass to within $4\times10^{-6}$.

\paragraph{Tasks.} SST-2, AG News, Bias in Bios (5 occupations) and EuroParl (5 languages), one
demonstration per class, on Gemma-2 and Gemma-3; a pair runs if pilot balanced accuracy is $\ge$0.70
(Appendix~\ref{app:stage5}).

\paragraph{Oracle.} The oracle $\tauj$ of a latent is its mean effect on held-out inputs (split O, 2,500 inputs,
\S\ref{sec:method}), and a latent \emph{matters} if $\tauj > \delta$. Knockoffs and observational e-values run on the
task's real labels and pooled raw-text activations, over 10 draws at $\alpha/C$ per class, with $C$ the number of
classes. Every latent they pick is intervened on and measured.

fixed and cross-validated penalties) select 19--71 latents per draw; none exceeds $\delta$, and 43--61\% have $\tauj > 2$ SE. Our
planned contrast favours hurdle: with the cross-validated penalty its picks more often have $\tauj > 2$ SE than block-MVR picks
($+0.07$, $p = 0.015$, 10 paired draws). This rests on SST-2 alone, the only task where both select, on draws that share data, and
hurdle trails equicorrelated picks ($-0.11$). At $q = 0.2$ a few picks on AG News and Gemma-3 EuroParl exceed $\delta$ (2--5\% of
picks, at most 6\% of the latents above $\delta$; on Gemma-3 AG News, one latent in one draw), against 0.4--3.6\% of measured latents.

\paragraph{The latent pool.} Knockoffs choose from the 2,048 most frequently firing latents. Of the 33 latents above $\delta$ on
the seven tasks, 25 lie in that pool, and knockoffs at $q/C$ pick none at $q = 0.1$, so the pool does not explain that miss. The other 8
fire too rarely to enter it; attribution screens all 16k. Recall here is among measured latents, while the share of picks above
$\delta$ has no such limit, because every pick was measured.

%=======================================================
\section{Interventional FDR Control}
\label{sec:method}
%=======================================================

We now turn the measurement of \S\ref{sec:downstream} into a selection procedure with a guarantee (Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline}).

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\figfile{0.9\textwidth}{fig_pipeline.pdf}
\caption{Architecture of interventional FDR control. Top: one latent is edited between the SAE's encoder and decoder, and the
drop in the correct-class probability is recorded. Bottom: how the data splits feed the procedure. Schematic.}
\label{fig:pipeline}
\end{figure*}

\paragraph{Procedure.} Disjoint splits: pilot (300), screening A (1,000), test B (2,500), oracle O
(2,500).
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item Attribution patching on A ranks all 16k latents by a first-order estimate of their effect; the top
        $K = 50$ become the hypotheses.
  \item On B, each hypothesis gets a betting e-value \citep{waudbysmith2024betting}. Starting with wealth 1,
        we observe the effects $\Delta_j(x_1), \dots, \Delta_j(x_n)$ one input at a time and bet a fraction
        $\lambda_t$ of the wealth that each exceeds $\delta$:
        \begin{equation}
        E_j = \prod_{t=1}^{n} \bigl(1 + \lambda_t(\Delta_j(x_t) - \delta)\bigr).
        \end{equation}
        The bet is \emph{predictable}: $\lambda_t$ depends only on $x_1, \dots, x_{t-1}$. We use aGRAPA,
        $\lambda_t = (\hat\mu - \delta)/(\hat\sigma^2 + (\hat\mu - \delta)^2)$ from the running mean and variance of past
        effects, with $\hat\mu$ shrunk towards $\delta$ and $\hat\sigma^2$ given a prior pseudo-observation of 0.25, clipped
        to $[0, 0.9/(1+\delta)]$ so each factor stays at least 0.1.
  \item e-BH \citep{wang2022evalues} over the $K$ e-values: sort them as $E_{(1)} \ge \dots \ge E_{(K)}$
        and reject the $k^*$ largest, $k^* = \max\{k : E_{(k)} \ge K/(\alpha
        k)\}$.
\end{enumerate}
Under $H_j$ each bet has a nonpositive expected return, so the wealth is a nonnegative supermartingale, $E_j$ is an e-value,
and e-BH controls FDR at $\alpha$ under any dependence. Assumptions: inputs are drawn at random, and split A is independent of B.

\paragraph{Validation.} The oracle $\tauj$ comes from held-out inputs. 200 replicates re-split B $\cup$ O
into $n$ test inputs (100--2,000) and an oracle. A \emph{stress test} shifts every $\Delta_j$ to mean
exactly $\delta$, so every rejection is false. Baselines on the same oracle: zero-null e-values (our procedure with $\delta = 0$),
attribution top-$k$ \citep{marks2025sparse} and the relative null of \citet{khera2026}.

is false, the rejection rate is at most 0.010.

\paragraph{Power.} We chose $K = 50$ and the bet cap 0.9 after seeing Gemma-2's results, so Gemma-2's power is
optimistic (the original $K = 250$, cap 0.5 gives 0.47--1.00 at $n = 2{,}000$; Table~\ref{tab:power-full}).
We then applied this setting unchanged to Gemma-3, the held-out model (Figure~\ref{fig:power}). There it beats the wider setting ($K = 250$, cap 0.5) on all four tasks at
$n = 1{,}000$ and on three of four at $n = 2{,}000$, tying on EuroParl. Power at small $n$ is limited (Table~\ref{tab:power-full}).
Zero and mean ablation give no breach, and their power is higher on most tasks (Table~\ref{tab:sensitivity}); each is scored against its own oracle, since the intervention changes which latents exceed $\delta$.

\paragraph{Few latents matter individually.} 59--91\% of candidates have a reliably positive effect, but
only 1--9 (up to 12 under ablation) exceed $\delta$ (Figure~\ref{fig:effects}); all of those rank in the attribution top 50. None of the 302 Gemma-2 and 77 Gemma-3 latents we measured outside the 250, mostly baseline picks, exceeds $\delta$.

\paragraph{A zero-effect null certifies negligible effects.} Its FDR against the minimum-effect truth
grows with $n$ (Figure~\ref{fig:zeronull}) while staying near 0 against its own target ($\tauj > 2$ SE).

\paragraph{Attribution top-$k$.} Keeping the top $k$ attribution scores finds most latents above $\delta$ (power 0.75--1.00),
but realised FDR is 0.10--0.90 at $k = 10$, 0.64--0.96 at $k = 25$ and 0.82--0.98 at $k = 50$, and nothing says which $k$ to use.

\paragraph{A relative null.} \citet{khera2026} shares our e-values and e-BH, but asks a different question: does ablating a
latent hurt more than ablating a random candidate, rather than does the effect exceed a fixed $\delta$? It also zeroes latents
instead of resampling them, measures a logit difference, and takes candidates active on at least 20\% of prompts; its
ground truth comes from a synthetic model. We reran its test on our candidates and metric, drawing the comparator from the
tested set (Table~\ref{tab:khera}), so this is a reimplementation, not a reproduction. Against its own target, its FDR is at most 0.002. The target itself depends on the candidate set: a candidate's mean effect is 1.2--5.7 times below $\delta$ among the
top 50 and 6--23 times below it among the 250, so on our tasks $\delta$ is the stricter bar. Neither exceeds $\alpha$ at $n \le 2{,}000$, though selections can differ (power 1.00 vs.\ 0.34 on Gemma-3 Bias in Bios, 0.28
vs.\ 0.61 on Gemma-3 AG News). At $n = 4{,}000$ the relative null exceeds $\alpha$ on two of eight tasks with $K = 50$ and on five with $K = 250$,
where its extra picks sit just below $\delta$ (0.79--0.86$\,\delta$ at $K = 50$), and it has more power near $\delta$; ours stays at or
below 0.002. Under zero ablation at $n = 1{,}000$ and $\alpha = 0.05$ with the same $K$, neither makes a false pick at cap 0.5, where ours has higher power on six tasks (one tie, one lower). At cap 0.9 the relative null's FDR reaches 0.033, and ours has higher power on three tasks (two ties, three lower).

%=======================================================
\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
%=======================================================

Observational SAE feature selection has two separate problems: a guarantee resting on a joint assumption none of our samplers
can be certified to meet, and a target of association rather than effect. The hurdle sampler shows these are distinct: it repairs
the zeros, yet its picks also rarely exceed $\delta$. Interventional e-BH needs neither assumption. The
minimum effect is the central choice: ``most screened latents have some effect'' and ``1--9 have a meaningful one'' are both true,
and a zero-effect null erases the difference. A relative null asks whether a latent stands out from its candidate set, an answer
that changes with the set; an absolute $\delta$ does not, at the cost of power near a cut-off we had to choose.

%=======================================================
\section{Conclusion}
%=======================================================

Search-then-validate can certify noise, knockoffs need an exchangeability SAE latents break, and better samplers improve the
per-latent match but still rarely pick latents above $\delta$. Testing interventions directly with betting e-values, e-BH and a minimum-effect null
controls the FDR of causal claims about latents without any assumption about their distribution, and shows that few of the
screened latents have a meaningful effect on their own.

%=======================================================
\section*{Limitations and Future Work}
%=======================================================

Models have 1--2B parameters, with one SAE each, and the interventional experiments use four tasks and one prompt format. Power is
relative to screened candidates, and single-latent tests miss jointly redundant latents. Steering was planned but not run, so we do
not test sufficiency. The comparison with \citet{khera2026} is a reimplementation, and its large-$n$ part uses resampling. $K$ and
the bet cap were tuned on Gemma-2, so only Gemma-3's power is held out. The audit uses planted labels on real latents. The downstream check on multi-class tasks is limited by the Knockoff+ selection floor, and its uncorrected picks come from one Gemma-2 draw with Gaussian knockoffs. Future work:
larger models, group testing of correlated latents, and other interventions such as steering.

\section{Planted-Signal Benchmark}
\label{app:planted}
%=======================================================

\paragraph{Knockoff procedure.} Latent $j$ is null if $Y \perp X_j \mid X_{-j}$. Knockoffs $\Xk$ are built
without $Y$ and satisfy $(X,\Xk)_{\mathrm{swap}(\mathcal{A})} \overset{d}{=} (X,\Xk)$ for every set $\mathcal{A}$ of latents. With
$W_j = |\hat\beta_j| - |\hat\beta_{\tilde j}|$ from an L1 logistic fit on $[X,\Xk]$, Knockoff+ selects
$\{j : W_j \ge \tau\}$ with
\begin{equation}
\tau = \min\Bigl\{t>0 : \tfrac{1 + |\{j : W_j \le -t\}|}{\max(1, |\{j: W_j \ge t\}|)} \le q\Bigr\}
\end{equation}

Signal latents $S$ are drawn at random; the logit is $a \cdot X_S w / \sqrt{k}$ with random signs $w$ (linear), or $0.7$ of
that plus $0.3$ of standardised pairwise products (interaction). Designs run from few strong signals ($k = 10, 30$) to many weak
ones ($k = 100, 300$), with amplitudes swept and matched to the probe AUC of real labels. A cell breaches when its mean FDR
exceeds $q$ significantly after BY correction. The fair-coin test takes, per replicate, the share of null latents with $W_j > 0$
among those with $W_j \ne 0$, and tests the mean against 0.5.

%=======================================================
\section{Hurdle and Deep Knockoff Samplers}
\label{app:samplers}
%=======================================================

\paragraph{Hurdle (SCIP).} For $j = 1, \dots, p$ in order, ridge models give $\Pr(X_j > 0 \mid X_{-j}, \Xk_{<j})$ and
$\log X_j \mid X_j > 0$, and $\Xk_j$ is drawn from them. SCIP is exact only if these are correct, so we check validity with the swap test.

\paragraph{Deep knockoffs.} The residual version adds a learned residual (two hidden layers of 1,024) to the block-MVR knockoff,
trained with swap MMD, moment matching and decorrelation losses \citep{romano2020deep}, cross-fitted over two folds. The
second-order-exact version maps learned noise on every draw to the Gaussian noise's mean and covariance and zero covariance with
$X$, so the network (width 256, early stopped) learns only third-order moments.

%=======================================================
\section{Observational e-Values}
\label{app:evalue}
%=======================================================

The half-B refit tests up to 500 lasso-selected latents with Wald $p$-values, which the calibrator
$e = \kappa p^{\kappa - 1}$ with $\kappa = 0.5$ turns into e-values. Lifting the cap lowered power ($-0.01$ to $-0.23$,
significant on 13 of 15 settings); averaging five splits gained a median $+0.04$ on SAEBench but lost $0.16$ on SST-2, and breached in one cell (Gemma-2 AG News) at all three targets (FDR $0.30, 0.36$ and $0.45$ at $q = 0.05, 0.1$ and $0.2$). On known-null latents the Wald $z$-scores have variance
1.69 with the cap and 5.17 without (nominal 1), and $|z| > 3.29$ occurs 11 times more often than nominal with the cap and about 150 times without; the
conservative calibration step is what kept the capped version within target.
