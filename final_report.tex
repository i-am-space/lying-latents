\begin{abstract}
%=======================================================
Interpretability work often reports that particular sparse autoencoder (SAE) latents drive a language
model's behaviour. Such claims need a selection that controls false discoveries and is causal. We audit
the standard selection methods on three models (Gemma-2-2B, Gemma-3-1B, Pythia-70M) and nine datasets.
Reusing data for search and validation certifies pure noise in 69--100\% of null experiments, and
held-out validation still in up to 27\% (nominal 5\%). Model-X knockoffs rest on exchangeability, which
SAE latents violate on every dataset: a classifier detects one swapped latent out of 2,048 at AUC
0.84--0.997. This becomes false discovery rate (FDR) breaches where labels depend on interactions, and
three samplers (Gaussian, hurdle, learned) each breach in some regime; the learned sampler looks best on
per-latent diagnostics and worst on FDR. Even valid observational methods test association with a label,
not use by the model. We propose \emph{interventional FDR control}: intervene on each candidate latent
inside the model and test whether its effect on the answer exceeds a minimum effect $\delta$, with
betting e-values and e-BH. The guarantee holds in finite samples, under any dependence, without a model
of the latents. On two models and four prompted tasks, realised FDR never exceeds 0.003, power reaches
0.34--1.00 at 2,000 inputs, a zero-effect null reaches FDR 0.60--0.96 against the minimum-effect truth,
and observational knockoffs find none of the latents that move the model. Only 1--9 of 250 screened
latents per task have a meaningful effect, although most have a small one.
\end{abstract}

%=======================================================
\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}
%=======================================================

Sparse autoencoders (SAEs) rewrite a language model's activations as sparse combinations of thousands of
learned directions, its \emph{latents}, many of which appear to track one concept
\citep{cunningham2023sparse, bricken2023monosemanticity, templeton2024scaling}. Claims built on them,
such as ``latent $j$ encodes sentiment and the model uses it'', are statistical claims made without
statistics. The standard recipe, \emph{search-then-validate}, ranks latents by association with a label,
keeps the top few and ablates them \citep{wang2023interpretability, marks2025sparse}. It tests thousands
of latents without multiplicity correction \citep{benjamini1995controlling} and often selects and
validates on the same data \citep{kriegeskorte2009circular}.

Model-X knockoffs \citep{candes2018panning}, applied to SAE latents with Gaussian decoys by
\citet{enkhbayar2025}, control the false discovery rate (FDR) if latents and decoys are exchangeable.
SAE latents are non-negative, exactly zero on most inputs and co-fire nonlinearly; Gaussian decoys are
never zero. We show the violation is detectable everywhere, turns into FDR breaches, and survives better
samplers. Moreover, even a valid knockoff answers an associational question, while interpretability
claims are causal. Interventions on our own model are free, exact and repeatable, so we test the causal
question directly.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item A systematic audit of SAE feature selection: 3 models, 9 datasets, 3 families of knockoff samplers (Gaussian, Hurdle, Learned) and
        e-value splitting (\S\ref{sec:audit}).
  \item Evidence that per-latent diagnostics mislead (\S\ref{sec:samplers}).
  \item Interventional e-BH with minimum-effect nulls: finite-sample FDR control for causal claims about
        latents, under arbitrary dependence (\S\ref{sec:method}).
  \item Validation against oracle effects on real models, with a stress test and a held-out-model
        confirmation (\S\ref{sec:results}).
  \item The finding that few latents matter individually (\S\ref{sec:results}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Background and Related Work}
\label{sec:background}
%=======================================================

\paragraph{SAEs.} JumpReLU SAEs \citep{rajamanoharan2024jumping, lieberum2024gemma} give each latent a
firing threshold, so its activations are a point mass at zero plus a positive part. SAE latents split
and absorb concepts \citep{chanin2024absorption}, score well even on random transformers
\citep{heap2025random}, and trail simple baselines on probing and steering
\citep{kantamneni2025sparse, wu2025axbench}; SAEBench evaluates SAEs as a whole
\citep{karvonen2025saebench}.

\paragraph{Interventions.} Components are validated by ablation or patching
\citep{wang2023interpretability, heimersheim2024patching}, which is sensitive to the metric
\citep{zhang2024towards} and to off-distribution edits \citep{makelov2024illusion}. Attribution patching
approximates many interventions with one gradient \citep{syed2023attribution, kramar2024atp} and is used
to select SAE latents \citep{marks2025sparse}. None of these controls the error rate of the reported set.

\paragraph{Multiple testing.} FDR is the expected share of false selections
\citep{benjamini1995controlling}; Benjamini--Yekutieli (BY) holds under any dependence
\citep{benjamini2001control}. Selecting hypotheses from the data that tests them is selective inference
\citep{berk2013valid, taylor2015statistical}.

\paragraph{Knockoffs.} Latent $j$ is null if $Y \perp X_j \mid X_{-j}$. Knockoffs $\Xk$ are built without
$Y$ and must satisfy $(X,\Xk)_{\mathrm{swap}(S)} \overset{d}{=} (X,\Xk)$ for every $S$
\citep{candes2018panning}. With $W_j = |\hat\beta_j| - |\hat\beta_{\tilde j}|$ from an L1 logistic fit on
$[X,\Xk]$, the Knockoff+ threshold
\begin{equation}
\tau = \min\Bigl\{t>0 : \tfrac{1 + |\{j : W_j \le -t\}|}{\max(1, |\{j: W_j \ge t\}|)} \le q\Bigr\}
\end{equation}
controls FDR at $q$ \citep{barber2015controlling}. FDR inflation under a wrong sampler is bounded by a
KL gap \citep{barber2020}. Samplers include second-order Gaussian (equicorrelated or MVR;
\citealp{spector2022powerful}), SCIP \citep{candes2018panning}, Metropolized \citep{bates2021metropolized}
and deep \citep{romano2020deep}.

\paragraph{E-values.} An e-value has expectation at most 1 under the null; e-BH controls FDR under any
dependence \citep{wang2022evalues}; betting gives e-values for bounded means
\citep{waudbysmith2024betting}.

\paragraph{SAE-specific work.} \citet{enkhbayar2025} use Gaussian knockoffs on SAE latents;
\citet{khera2026} combine ablation e-processes with e-BH under a zero-effect null; \citet{bal2026}
report many causally inert SAE features. We add a minimum-effect null, on-manifold interventions,
oracle-verified FDR on real models, and the audit.

$k$ random latents (10--300; linear or pairwise-interaction logits). Each method also runs on a
\emph{Gaussian control} with the real covariance, where Gaussian knockoffs are valid. Penalties are
chosen by 5-fold cross-validation (Appendix~\ref{app:planted}).

about 1\% of the noise and it behaves like block MVR. FDR is a joint property of 2,048 dependent latents,
and no sampler we built can be certified jointly.

\subsection{The valid fallback is costly}
\label{sec:evalue-obs}

E-value splitting (lasso selection on one half, a logistic refit on the other, e-BH) never breached
(14 model--dataset settings, FDR 0.000--0.008) but has 0.27--0.46 less power than hurdle knockoffs (0.60 on the smallest dataset).
Its null $z$-scores are over-dispersed (variance 1.69), so its validity is empirical
(Appendix~\ref{app:evalue}).

\paragraph{Summary.} No observational method is both valid and powerful on SAE latents, and all target
association with a label rather than effect on the model.

%=======================================================
\section{Interventional FDR Control}
\label{sec:method}
%=======================================================

\paragraph{Target.} For a prompted classification task, $m(x)$ is the model's probability of the
correct class among the label tokens. The effect of latent $j$ is
\begin{equation}
\Delta_j(x) = m(x) - m(x;\ \text{intervene on } j) \in [-1, 1],
\end{equation}
$\tauj = \mathbb{E}_x[\Delta_j(x)]$, and we test $H_j: \tauj \le \delta$, with $\delta = 0.05\,\mathrm{SD}(m)$
on a pilot split (0.008--0.016). A zero-effect null is avoided because almost every latent moves the
output slightly (\S\ref{sec:results}). The claim is causal within the model's computation, for these
prompts; redundant latents can be missed.

\paragraph{Intervention.} On every query token at the SAE layer, $h \leftarrow h + (\tilde a_j - a_j)
d_j$, with $d_j$ the decoder vector, so the SAE error is kept. The primary $\tilde a_j$ is a draw from a
token-level hurdle model of $a_j$ given its 64 most co-active latents; zero and mean ablation are
sensitivity analyses. Validity does not depend on this sampler. Only layers after $L$ are replayed,
matching the full forward pass within $4\times10^{-6}$.

\paragraph{Procedure.} Disjoint splits: pilot (300), screening A (1,000), test B (2,500), oracle O
(2,500).
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=1pt,topsep=2pt]
  \item Attribution patching on A ranks all 16k latents; the top $K = 50$ are hypotheses.
  \item On B, a betting e-value \citep{waudbysmith2024betting} per hypothesis,
        \begin{equation}
        E_j = \prod_{t=1}^{n} \bigl(1 + \lambda_t(\Delta_j(x_t) - \delta)\bigr),
        \end{equation}
        with predictable aGRAPA bets $\lambda_t \in [0, 0.9/(1+\delta)]$.
  \item e-BH \citep{wang2022evalues}: reject the $k^*$ largest, $k^* = \max\{k : E_{(k)} \ge K/(\alpha
        k)\}$.
\end{enumerate}
Under $H_j$ the wealth is a nonnegative supermartingale, so $E_j$ is an e-value and e-BH controls FDR
at $\alpha$ under any dependence. Assumptions: random inputs and A independent of B.

\paragraph{Validation.} The oracle $\tauj$ comes from held-out inputs. 200 replicates re-split B $\cup$ O
into $n$ test inputs (100--2,000) and an oracle. A \emph{stress test} shifts every $\Delta_j$ to mean
exactly $\delta$, so every rejection is false. Baselines: zero-null e-values (as \citealp{khera2026}),
attribution top-$k$ \citep{marks2025sparse}, Gaussian knockoffs \citep{enkhbayar2025} and observational
e-values, the last two over 10 draws at $\alpha/C$ per class.

\paragraph{Tasks.} SST-2, AG News, Bias in Bios (5 occupations) and EuroParl (5 languages), one
demonstration per class, on Gemma-2 and Gemma-3; a pair runs if pilot balanced accuracy is $\ge$0.70
(Appendix~\ref{app:stage5}).

%=======================================================
\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}
%=======================================================

Observational SAE feature selection is unreliable twice over: its guarantee rests on a joint
distributional assumption no sampler we built satisfies, and its target is association rather than
effect. Interventional e-BH needs neither. The minimum effect is central: ``most latents have some
effect'' and ``1--9 have a meaningful one'' are both true, and a zero-effect null erases the
difference. Future work: larger models, group testing of correlated latents, and other interventions
such as steering.

%=======================================================
\section{Conclusion}
%=======================================================

The standard pipeline certifies noise, knockoffs need an exchangeability SAE latents break, and valid
observational fallbacks are weak and associational. Testing interventions directly with betting
e-values, e-BH and a minimum-effect null controls the FDR of causal claims about latents without any
model of their distribution, and shows that few SAE latents matter on their own.

%=======================================================
\section*{Limitations}
%=======================================================

Models have 1--2B parameters, with one SAE each. The interventional experiments use four tasks and one prompt format. Power is
relative to screened candidates, single-latent tests miss jointly redundant latents, and power at small
$n$ is limited. Observational baselines see pooled raw-text activations of 2,048 latents. $K$ and the bet
cap were tuned on Gemma-2. The audit uses planted labels on real latents.

\section{Planted-Signal Benchmark}
\label{app:planted}
%=======================================================

Signal latents $S$ are drawn at random; the logit is $a \cdot X_S w / \sqrt{k}$ with random signs $w$
(linear), or $0.7$ of that plus $0.3$ of standardised pairwise products (interaction), centred for
class balance. Designs range from few strong signals ($k = 10, 30$) to many weak ones
($k = 100, 300$), with amplitudes swept and matched to the probe AUC of real labels. Knockoff+ runs at
$q \in \{0.05, 0.1, 0.2\}$; a cell breaches when its mean FDR exceeds $q$ significantly after BY
correction across cells. The fair-coin test computes, per replicate, the share of null latents with
$W_j > 0$ among those with $W_j \ne 0$, and tests the mean over replicates against 0.5.

%=======================================================
\section{Hurdle and Deep Knockoff Samplers}
\label{app:samplers}
%=======================================================

\paragraph{Hurdle (SCIP).} For $j = 1, \dots, p$ in order, a ridge logistic model gives
$\Pr(X_j > 0 \mid X_{-j}, \Xk_{<j})$ and a ridge linear model gives $\log X_j \mid X_j > 0$ (log-normal
sizes); $\Xk_j$ is drawn from the fitted conditionals. SCIP is exact only if the conditionals are
correct; ours are estimated, so validity is checked by the swap test.

\paragraph{Deep knockoffs.} The residual version adds a learned residual (two hidden layers of 1,024) to the block-MVR
Gaussian knockoff and trains it with swap MMD, moment matching and decorrelation losses
\citep{romano2020deep}, cross-fitted over two row folds. The second-order-exact version replaces the Gaussian noise with a
learned noise that is mapped on every draw to exactly the Gaussian noise's mean and covariance and
zero covariance with $X$, so the second moments are exact by construction; the network (width 256,
early stopped) learns only third-order exchangeability moments. Trained on the Gaussian control, it
does not damage a valid starting point.

$e = \kappa p^{\kappa - 1}$ with $\kappa = 0.5$. Lifting the cap lowered power ($-0.01$ to $-0.23$,
